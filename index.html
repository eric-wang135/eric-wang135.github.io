Making biomedical signals reliable in the real world

I develop deep learning methods for physiological signal enhancement, signal quality assessment, and super-resolution, with a focus on surface electromyography (sEMG) and electrocardiography (ECG).

A central theme of my work is that reconstruction accuracy is not the same as physiological validity: a denoiser can lower waveform error while quietly distorting the spectral features clinicians actually read. I build physiological priors directly into the model, and pair enhancement with quality assessment so a signal's trustworthiness can be judged without a clean reference — the situation in every real clinical and wearable recording.

Featured Research

sEMG Enhancement
sEMG Enhancement and Denoising
My longest-running line of work: removing ECG artifacts from sEMG, where the two signals overlap in frequency and naive filtering destroys the muscle activity it is meant to preserve. The line runs from fully convolutional denoisers through score-based diffusion, representation-masking Transformers, and efficient Mamba networks, and has most recently moved into the time–frequency domain with physiologically informed sub-band masking — so that the spectral features downstream analysis depends on survive the enhancement.

Signal Quality Assessment
sEMG Signal Quality Assessment
Neural quality-assessment methods that estimate sEMG usability without a clean reference signal — a prerequisite for trusting enhanced signals in clinical and wearable settings, where ground truth is never available.

ECG Super-Resolution
ECG Super-Resolution
Reconstructing high-resolution ECG from low-sampling-rate recordings, so wearable devices can save power without giving up diagnostic fidelity. Recent work applies residual-shifting conditional diffusion to hold up under distribution shift across datasets, where discriminative models degrade.
